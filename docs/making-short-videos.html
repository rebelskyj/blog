On Making Short Videos

First Published: 12 August 2026

Draft 1: 12 August 2026

In the past, I’ve realized that something I find really important is science communication. I’ve written here about how I want to teach rotational spectroscopy. That’s something that almost certainly requires a textbook, problems, and lectures. However, I cannot force that onto people.

The textbook I can certainly write with the lectures, and the problems I can generate as feel relevant. However, the videos are really where I hit my biggest issue. I know that I am incredibly shy of recording, and will spend forever trying to make any recording perfect. Add to that the fact that I don’t entirely know how to make animations well, and you end up with a great recipe for procrastination and lack of doing.1

However, I’m realizing that there’s another important issue to making rotational spectroscopy videos: getting people to look at them and learn. I know that it’s important to meet people where they’re at. I’m not going to begin with assumptions that people know upper-level undergraduate chemistry or mathematics2. But, I have been assuming that people will be watching these long videos on YouTube or some other long-form site.

From what I understand, this is not the nature of modern media consumption.

Rather than fight the trend, then, I want to spend some time considering short form content that I could make to explain small concepts. If I have those, I can then link them into longer videos that explain a full idea. Goal of this page is, then, to have short ideas that I like and to ideate. It’s again going to be held in a secondary list location.3

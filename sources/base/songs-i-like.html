Note: Current plan is to go through my favorites list from most to least recent.

  1. “Burn the Deck” Slothrust Burn the Deck - Single (Added to this list: 11 August 2026 (entry 3))

    • Maybe the weekly discovery list? Or else shuffling with discover on

    • I like the lyricism, and I really like the driving guitar between lyrical sections, along with the guitar in it

    • Probably the stabs of the vocals with the guitar between them

    • Angry, emo, rock

    • The lack of fear of having no lyrics for long moments, along with the lack of fear to drop whole swaths of the soundscape

      Confidence in singing, the guitar stabbing very intentionally

  2. “Leave Her Johnny” The Wellermen Northern Sky (Added to this list: 11 August 2026 (entry 2))

    • As with Santiana

    • I really like the harmonies, and I generally really like the song, regardless of harmonization.

    • Probably lyrics, since they’re slightly different than any other version I’ve heard, otherwise the extended chords at the ending choruses

    • Sad call and response modern sea shanty (so one person sings a line, group joins, then all in chorus, good harmony)

    • The way that they work with the different harmonizations of the same chorus

      Blend as always, otherwise, the way that the lead singer enunciates

  3. “Santiana (feat. the Brass Gang” The Wellermen & Piper Ally Northern Sky (Added to this list: 11 August 2026 (entry 1))

    • It was in the album club I chose this week (starting 9 August 2026). I saw the group initially from “Torn Screen Door”, which I imagine came in the weekly discovery queue

    • I like the subtle bagpipes1, and I really like the shift to the final verse’s harmony. I also generally like the harmonies through the song. The bit about 30 seconds from the end of the song also have some really cool synths

    • Probably the repetition of “Heave her up and away we’ll go, away Santiana” at the end of the song

    • Modern Sea Shanty (so slightly more harmonic, less haulable to)

    • The vocal harmonies and the way that they layer other instrumentation later on

      Not much, the blend is pretty basic advice, but always good to remember vocal blend
